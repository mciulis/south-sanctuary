# South Sanctuary — Copywriting Guide

---

## Tone of Voice

### The feeling we're after
Reading copy on this site should feel like being shown around a home by someone who deeply loves it — not a real estate agent reciting features, but an owner who knows every corner and what it means. Quiet confidence. Specific details. Sensory language that lets the reader feel the space before they see it.

### Adjectives that guide us
Peaceful. Intentional. Warm. Considered. Private. Alive. Unhurried.

### What we avoid
- Real estate listing language ("spacious open floor plan," "must see")
- Hollow superlatives ("stunning," "beautiful," "amazing" used lazily)
- Overselling or pressure
- Rushed sentences — the pace of the copy should mirror the pace of the home

### What we lean into
- Specificity over generality ("11 south-facing windows" beats "lots of natural light")
- Sensory detail (the sound of the creek, the quality of afternoon light, the warmth of a tile roof)
- First or second person — intimate, not institutional
- Short sentences that breathe, occasionally followed by a longer one that opens up
- Mixing all-caps serif headers with lowercase italic for emphasis (mirroring the visual style)

### Reference phrases from the template (Selene)
- "ELEVATED *SPATIAL* DESIGN" — bold noun + italic qualifier
- "LET'S GET IN TOUCH" — direct but warm call to action
- Body copy: short paragraphs, 3–4 sentences max, generous white space around them

## Voice Examples

**Instead of:** "Beautiful modern living area with south facing views of native forest"
**Write:** "Eleven windows face south. The forest doesn't ask permission — it simply fills the room."

**Instead of:** "Stunning deck with full patio furniture"
**Write:** "Eight hundred and fifty square feet of deck. Multi-tiered, newly renewed, with a gas line already run to the grill. Private in every direction."

**Instead of:** "Master bathroom inspired by a coffee shop in Bratislava"
**Write:** "The master bathroom was designed after a café we stumbled into in Bratislava — a place called Fach. In the middle of the city, it was a still, tranquil respite. That's what we wanted here."

## Draft Copy — Story Page

*To be written section by section as we build. Draft here first, then move into code.*

### Hero Headline (2–3 words + italic qualifier)
Options:
- "A QUIET *SANCTUARY*"
- "SOUTH *SANCTUARY*"
- "TUCKED *INTO THE TREES*"

### Hero Subheadline
"230 S Canby — Portland, Oregon"

### Intro Paragraph (draft)
> There's a certain kind of house that doesn't announce itself.
> South Sanctuary sits at the end of a quiet street, the city just minutes away, the forest pressed close behind it.
> It asks nothing of you. It simply holds you.

### The Setting (draft)
> A dead-end street with no overhead lines — everything buried, nothing interrupting the sky.
> A small creek runs through the property beyond the trees. On still mornings, you can hear it.
> The lots on this side of the street are double the size of those across from it. Space, here, is not a luxury. It is the premise.

### The Arrival (draft)
> Custom glass railings greet you at the entry — a quiet architectural gesture that frames what's ahead.
> As you descend to the main floor, the room opens. Eleven windows face south. The light that comes through them is not fixed; it moves through the day, through the seasons, always soft, always changing.

### The Kitchen (draft — to be expanded)
> The island is wide enough for two people cooking and four people gathering around.
> A fireplace sits at one end of the kitchen. The dining table belongs here too.

### The Deck (draft)
> Eight hundred and fifty square feet. Multi-tiered.
> A direct gas line means you never run out — the grill is always ready.
> The deck was just renewed: boards replaced, cleaned, painted.
> Private in every direction. A place you won't want to leave.

### The Master Suite (draft)
> The master bathroom was designed after a café we found in Bratislava — a place called Fach.
> In the middle of a noisy city, it was absolutely still.
> That stillness is what we wanted here.

### Transition to Estate Sale (draft)
> Everything inside has a story, too.
> The furniture — collected over years from makers we love — is available to its next home.
